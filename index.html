Jennifer Hsia

I am a final-year PhD candidate in Machine Learning at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Aarti Singh and Zachary Lipton. Before CMU, I received a B.S.E. in Computer Science from Princeton University, where I was advised by Peter Ramadge.

I study the reliability of large language models: when models reach conclusions their inputs don't justify, and how to catch and fix it. My work examines how models handle users' claims about themselves, how they choose among plausible answers when retrieved evidence favors the wrong one, and whether standard evaluations actually measure what they claim to. I design controlled evaluations that expose these failures and develop methods that reduce them.

Previously, I was a Research Scientist Intern at Meta (Ads Ranking), working on compact representations of long user-behavior histories.

I am on the job market for industry research roles in LLM evaluation, model behavior, and reliability, graduating in May 2027.

Joint Constraint Aggregation for Qualifier-Sensitive Retrieval-Augmented QA

Jennifer Hsia, Zachary Lipton, Aarti Singh

EMNLP 2026  /  arXiv  /  Code

LLMs can be pulled toward plausible answers that miss a key qualifier in the question when retrieval favors them. JCA checks every candidate against the question's requirements and compares them side by side, improving both accuracy and robustness to misleading retrieval.

ICML 2025  /  Proceedings  /  arXiv  /  Code  /  BibTeX

Evaluating RAG at a single retrieval depth hides brittleness: as more passages are added, some models degrade while others plateau, a pattern that tracks each model's sensitivity to distracting passages more than the choice of retriever.
