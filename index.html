Jennifer Hsia

Hello! I am a Ph.D. student in the Machine Learning department at Carnegie Mellon University. I am advised by Prof. Aarti Singh and Prof. Zachary Lipton.

My research studies how language models fail to use retrieved information appropriately in question answering. My work examines how noise sensitivity and frequency bias cause models to select incorrect answers even when relevant evidence is present, and asks how models should handle questions that are themselves underspecified or false.

I graduated with a B.S.E. in Computer Science from Princeton University. My research was advised by Prof. Peter Ramadge.

Research
Deciding Among Plausible Answers: Joint Constraint Aggregation for Retrieval-Augmented QA
Jennifer Hsia, Zachary Lipton, Aarti Singh
Under review

When retrieved passages surface multiple plausible candidates, models tend to select the most frequently mentioned one even when it violates a qualifier in the question. We decompose the question into atomic constraints, evaluate each candidate against each constraint using retrieved evidence, and jointly aggregate across candidates to select the answer that best satisfies what the question requires.

RAGGED: Towards Informed Design of Scalable and Stable RAG Systems
Jennifer Hsia, Afreen Shaikh, Zhiruo Wang, Graham Neubig
ICML 2025
Paper / Code / bibtex

Reader robustness to noisy retrieval, not retriever quality, is the key bottleneck for RAG stability and scalability. This pattern persists across retrievers, rerankers, and prompts.

Goodhart’s Law Applies to NLP Explanation Benchmarks
Jennifer Hsia, Aarti Singh, Danish Pruthi, Zachary Lipton
EACL 2024
Paper / Code / bibtex

Simple wrapper methods can inflate faithfulness scores dramatically without improving the underlying explanations, showing that current metrics are not suited to guide explainability research.
