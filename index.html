Jennifer Hsia

I am a final-year PhD candidate in Machine Learning at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Aarti Singh and Zachary Lipton. Before CMU, I received a B.S.E. in Computer Science from Princeton University, where I was advised by Peter Ramadge.

I study language model behavior and evaluation, with a focus on grounded systems. My research asks why models fail even when the information they need is available, develops targeted evaluations to identify those failures, and explores interventions that help models use evidence more reliably. My recent work examines how grounded assistants should reason about the assumptions embedded in a user's question before answering it.

I am currently on the market for industry roles focused on model behavior, evaluation, and reliable grounded systems.

Deciding Among Plausible Answers: Joint Constraint Aggregation for Retrieval-Augmented QA

Jennifer Hsia, Zachary Lipton, Aarti Singh

EMNLP 2026

When retrieval surfaces several plausible answers, models often favor the most frequently supported candidate even when it violates a qualifier in the question. JCA decomposes questions into explicit constraints and jointly aggregates evidence across candidates.
